# Relevo Studio — Website Experience Brief v1

## Status

Approved experience direction for the first Relevo Studio public website.

This brief translates the strategic context, the current commercial proposal pattern, the approved visual system, and the Dala reference experience into implementation-facing experience principles.

It defines the **job of the website and the direction of the experience**, not final copy, final information architecture, or the production technology stack.

Read together with:

- `context/relevo-strategy.md` for strategic positioning;
- `design-system/` for the visual source of truth;
- `AGENTS.md` for implementation guardrails.

## 1. What the website has to achieve

The first Relevo Studio website is not primarily a service catalog or a conventional lead-generation landing page.

Its main job is to become a **credibility artifact and experiential proof** for people who encounter Relevo through outreach, referrals, proposals, demos, or direct conversations.

The desired reaction is approximately:

> These people clearly understand product, technology, AI and operations. This feels like a world-class studio, and they can actually build.

The experience should feel comparable in ambition and craft to a top independent technology studio in Berlin, London or New York, while remaining unmistakably Relevo.

The site must do four things:

1. **Create immediate premium credibility.** The first seconds should feel technically sophisticated, restrained and intentional.
2. **Make the Relevo thesis understandable without over-explaining it.** Relevo finds operational friction and turns it into intelligent systems that work.
3. **Show evidence of how Relevo thinks and builds.** Concrete diagnosis and working systems matter more than a generic list of capabilities.
4. **End in an intelligent conversation.** The primary conversion experience should feel like a demonstration of the Relevo thesis, not a generic contact form.

## 2. Primary audience and context of use

The primary audience for v1 is a decision-maker evaluating whether Relevo is credible enough to trust with an important operational or commercial problem.

Typical visitors may include founders, owners, general managers, commercial leaders, operations leaders, digital/e-commerce leaders and other executives in functioning Latin American companies where meaningful work still depends on manual responses, fragmented tools, repeated decisions or person-dependent workflows.

Many visitors will be **warm prospects**. They may arrive after:

- receiving an outreach message;
- receiving a Relevo proposal;
- seeing a working demo;
- being referred by someone;
- hearing about Relevo in a business conversation.

Therefore the site must be able to carry trust after a proposal. It should not feel like a generic agency landing page that weakens the quality of the commercial material that sent the visitor there.

## 3. The commercial pattern the website should embody

Current Relevo proposals reveal a consistent method:

1. Inspect the business before prescribing a solution.
2. Find the real point where a sale, decision, workflow or piece of knowledge gets stuck.
3. Frame the problem in operational and economic terms.
4. Design a system rather than an isolated feature.
5. Combine people, data, software, workflows and AI where each belongs.
6. Build enough to demonstrate the idea concretely.
7. Deliver a working system rather than stopping at a presentation.

Two commercial ideas are especially important to the website narrative:

> No es trabajo de una persona. Es trabajo de un sistema.

and

> Entregamos el sistema funcionando, no la presentación.

These are strategic signals, not necessarily final homepage copy.

The website should make visitors feel this method before it explains it explicitly.

## 4. Core narrative idea

The central experience metaphor is:

**complexity becoming a working system.**

The site can begin with information, work, decisions and conversations as dispersed elements. Through the experience, those elements become related, coordinated and purposeful.

The narrative should move conceptually through:

**weight / friction → understanding → orchestration → system → evidence → conversation**

This connects naturally with the Relevo name and the idea of `relevare`: making lighter what weighs.

Do not explain this metaphor literally on every screen. The visitor should primarily **feel** it through motion, pacing and structure.

## 5. Dala as the primary experience reference

Dala is the main reference for the **grammar of the experience**, not for Relevo's visual identity or source assets.

The following characteristics are explicitly desirable to borrow and reinterpret:

- a persistent visual world that continues across most or all of the page;
- a deep particle field with spatial depth and parallax;
- particles that can aggregate into, dissolve from and morph between forms;
- a central visual protagonist that changes state across the narrative;
- scroll acting as a scrubber for a reversible timeline rather than only triggering one-off animations;
- cinematic transitions between chapters rather than hard section-to-section cuts;
- strong use of darkness and negative space;
- moments where the visual system becomes spectacular and moments where it deliberately becomes quiet;
- semantic HTML content layered over or around the persistent scene;
- transformations that make one visual state become the next instead of resetting the canvas;
- a closing transition that resolves the visual story and leads naturally into the conversion experience.

A brain-like or organic particle sculpture may be explored if it is valuable to the concept, but it must be an original Relevo asset and not a reproduction of Dala's exact sculpture, silhouette, sequence or composition.

Do **not** copy:

- Dala source code;
- proprietary assets or models;
- exact object geometry;
- exact sequence of objects;
- exact composition, choreography, timings or camera paths;
- copy, typography or brand identity;
- a pixel-for-pixel page structure.

The target is to achieve a similarly ambitious class of experience while producing a distinct Relevo work.

## 6. Meaning of the particle system

Particles are not decoration. They are the main generative material of the experience.

Conceptually they can represent:

- work;
- information;
- decisions;
- conversations;
- operational actions;
- knowledge moving between people and systems.

Their behavior should communicate changes in organization:

- dispersed;
- blocked;
- attracted;
- transferred;
- connected;
- coordinated;
- structured;
- responsive.

The exact particle geometry and protagonist forms remain to be designed.

The Relevo rings and the idea of one element handing continuity to another may inform motion, transitions, transfer and topology, but the logo should not be stretched into a literal decorative motif everywhere.

## 7. Preliminary narrative acts

This is a storytelling framework, not a locked sitemap.

### Act 0 — Arrival / intrigue

Goal: establish quality before explanation.

A dark, highly controlled opening. Sparse copy. The particle world is already alive.

The initial proposition should be conceptual and confident rather than a paragraph explaining services.

The visitor should immediately understand that Relevo is a serious technology studio, while still wanting to discover what the studio actually does.

### Act 1 — The weight

Goal: make the problem recognizable without presenting a list of pain points.

Show the reality of functioning organizations where important work still depends on manual replies, fragmented information, repeated decisions, handoffs and people holding process knowledge in their heads.

The particle system can become more distributed, constrained, interrupted or inefficient.

### Act 2 — Find the point of friction

Goal: communicate that Relevo diagnoses before prescribing.

Relevo does not arrive with a fixed catalog of AI products. It investigates where the business actually stops, slows down or loses conversion.

This act can begin to reveal relationships and patterns inside the apparent complexity.

### Act 3 — Build the system

Goal: express the Relevo thesis at maximum visual clarity.

The visual world begins to coordinate. Elements that were dispersed become a working system.

This is the natural territory for the idea:

> No es trabajo de una persona. Es trabajo de un sistema.

The experience should communicate that a Relevo system can combine software, AI, data, workflows, interfaces and human judgment without reducing the proposition to "we build chatbots" or "we automate tasks."

### Act 4 — Evidence

Goal: convert abstraction into credibility.

Visual spectacle should become quieter and give concrete evidence more space.

The first site should favor short, specific proof over a conventional logo wall or generic portfolio grid.

Useful evidence patterns from current commercial work include examples such as:

- hundreds of product records audited systematically and turned into an ongoing quality system;
- a technical buying journey where repeated questions become a responsive advisor, calculator and qualified-opportunity path;
- diagnosing that a conversion problem is actually a decision problem rather than a traffic problem;
- building a functional demonstration before asking the customer to commit to the full project.

Do not publish client names, confidential data or outcome claims without explicit approval. A proposal finding is not automatically a public case study or verified business result.

### Act 5 — The studio / method

Goal: explain enough about Relevo to make the capability legible.

This should remain concise. Avoid the standard agency pattern of large service-card grids such as Strategy / Design / Development / AI.

The method can be communicated through a small number of verbs or states such as:

**understand → design → build → operate**

Exact wording is not approved yet.

The origin of the Relevo name (`relevare`, making lighter what weighs) may be used if it strengthens the story rather than becoming forced brand mythology.

### Act 6 — Conversation

Goal: make the conversion mechanism itself feel like Relevo.

The final visual transition should lead into an agent-led conversation rather than ending abruptly in a standard footer form.

The experience should feel as though the visual system has become interactive and is now engaging directly with the visitor.

## 8. Conversion experience: Relevo agent

The preferred primary conversion for the site is an intelligent conversational experience.

### Public diagnosis mode — v1 direction

A public visitor should be able to start with the problem rather than filling a form.

The agent should behave like a lightweight version of the Relevo discovery method. It can ask about:

- what process or commercial moment is causing friction;
- what is currently done manually;
- who participates;
- which tools or systems are involved;
- approximate volume or frequency;
- what happens when nobody responds or acts;
- what outcome the company wants to improve.

The conversation should then be able to summarize what it understood and, where appropriate, propose a **system hypothesis**, not a definitive solution pretending to know facts it does not have.

A useful end state may be conceptually similar to:

- what we understood;
- where the likely friction is;
- what a system could potentially do;
- what we would need to understand next.

Then it should capture the minimum contact information needed to continue the conversation.

A conventional contact method should remain available as a fallback for accessibility, preference and reliability.

### Proposal-aware mode — future capability

A later version may allow a prospect to arrive from a Relevo proposal and converse with an agent that has approved context for that specific proposal.

The agent could explain:

- the diagnosis;
- proposed scope;
- phases;
- assumptions;
- exclusions;
- investment;
- open questions;
- related demos.

This should use a secure, non-guessable proposal link or token and strict context isolation. A simple sequential quotation number must not be treated as sufficient access control.

This is a future capability, not a requirement to block the first visual prototype.

## 9. Product visibility

The v1 homepage should sell the **studio capability**, not a portfolio of Relevo products.

- Do not create a Products section by default.
- Do not mention Workieo anywhere on the public Relevo Studio website.
- Serchi and Cauvia may exist as internal evidence of the studio's product-building capability, but should remain absent from the v1 public homepage unless explicitly approved later.
- Do not infer or publish a corporate relationship between Cauvia and Relevo beyond current approved source-of-truth material.

The absence of a product section is intentional, not missing content.

## 10. Content and copy behavior

The page should balance two modes.

### Cinematic / conceptual

Use at major narrative transitions:

- short statements;
- strong typography;
- large negative space;
- restrained explanation;
- language about systems, motion, work, decisions and operations.

### Concrete / evidentiary

Use when credibility needs to become specific:

- numbers;
- observed problems;
- short before/after system descriptions;
- clear operational outcomes;
- explicit limits and honest caveats.

Do not let abstract AI language replace concrete business meaning.

Avoid the conventional agency tone of "we combine strategy, design and technology to unlock transformation" unless the statement is made specific enough to mean something.

Final homepage copy is not approved by this document.

## 11. Experience architecture principles

The implementation should be designed around a **persistent scene**, not a collection of unrelated section animations.

Conceptually:

```text
semantic DOM content
        ↓
normalized page / chapter progress
        ↓
scene controller
        ↓
persistent visual world
(camera, particles, geometry, lighting, morph states)
```

Key principles:

- scroll progress should be able to map continuously to scene state;
- important transformations should be reversible when the user scrolls backward;
- the visual protagonist should persist across chapters where possible;
- content sections should be semantic DOM, selectable, indexable and accessible rather than being rendered as text inside the 3D scene;
- visual intensity should be choreographed; not every chapter should be equally spectacular;
- quiet evidence moments are part of the cinematic rhythm;
- the scene should never make reading or interacting with content unnecessarily difficult.

No framework, WebGL library, animation library, smooth-scroll library or rendering approach is approved by this brief.

## 12. Mobile, performance and accessibility are first-class

The visual concept must be designed with degradation in mind from the beginning.

Requirements:

- support `prefers-reduced-motion` with a deliberately designed reduced-motion experience;
- preserve the narrative when heavy particle behavior is reduced or disabled;
- plan mobile visual states rather than shrinking the desktop composition after implementation;
- keep semantic content independent from the rendering scene;
- ensure the agent can be used without requiring motion or 3D interaction;
- avoid making critical information dependent exclusively on animation;
- design particle count, pixel density, effects and asset complexity around realistic performance budgets.

Exact performance budgets will be defined with the production architecture.

## 13. What success should feel like

The first website is successful if:

- the first five seconds create a strong perception of craft and technical depth;
- a prospect can understand Relevo's type of work within roughly thirty seconds without reading a service catalog;
- the site feels coherent from first frame to final CTA rather than like stacked sections with effects;
- abstract storytelling is eventually grounded in specific evidence;
- the experience increases, rather than reduces, confidence after receiving a Relevo proposal;
- the final agent interaction feels like proof of the product philosophy;
- the site remains credible and usable when motion is reduced or the device is constrained.

- which commercial examples are approved for public publication;
- whether client names can be shown;

- final web framework;

These decisions should be made deliberately after the narrative and visual prototype are tested, not silently chosen by an implementation agent.
